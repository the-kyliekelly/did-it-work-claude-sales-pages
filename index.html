the whole point of testing

Did It Work?

A published page tells you nothing on its own. Here's how to actually see what happened — and what to do with what you find.

why this step matters

Zero sales can mean two very different things

It can mean nobody wanted the offer. Or it can mean almost nobody actually saw the page. Without a way to check visits, you can't tell the difference — and you might kill a good offer because of a traffic problem, or keep believing in one that nobody's even looking at.

step one

Get a basic visit count

You don't need anything complicated. Pick one of these before you send traffic:

Easiest

Free analytics snippet

GoatCounter or Cloudflare Web Analytics — both free, no cookie banner needed, one script tag.

  1. Sign up free, grab your snippet
  2. Ask Claude to add it before </body>
  3. Re-upload to GitHub
  4. Check your dashboard after a day or two

A link shortener

Bitly or similar. Shorten your page link before sharing it and see total clicks — no HTML editing needed, though you won't see what happened after the click.

Your checkout's own data

ThriveCart and Teachery sometimes show page views if the button links straight to a hosted checkout. Worth checking before adding anything new.

step two

Know what you're comparing

Once you have a visit count and a sales count, the number that actually matters is the rate between them — not either number on its own.

MetricWhat it tells you
VisitsReach — did the page get in front of people at all
Checkout clicks ÷ visitsInterest — did the offer make people want to buy
Purchases ÷ checkout clicksFriction — did anything at checkout stop them
A rough rule of thumb — for a warm, no-social audience landing on a cold-launched page like this, a healthy checkout-click rate is often somewhere in the low-to-mid single digits (a handful out of every 100 visits). It varies a lot by price, audience warmth, and offer, so treat this as a loose sanity check, not a hard target — the trend against your own past offers matters more than hitting a specific number.

step three

Decide what happens next

Set your own line before you look at the numbers, so you're not talking yourself into whatever result you get.

Worth building out further
  • People are visiting and clicking through to checkout
  • You're getting sales, or close-but-no-purchase clicks with clear interest
  • Any objections you're hearing are answerable, not fundamental
Rework or shelve it
  • Good traffic, but almost no checkout clicks — the offer itself isn't landing
  • Checkout clicks but no purchases — look at price, trust, or checkout friction first
  • Barely any visits at all — this is a traffic problem, not an offer problem; retest with more reach before judging the offer
